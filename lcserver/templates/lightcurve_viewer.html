Time Axis

Magnitudes - the photometry of every source as published, or as converted to a common band where the source is listed with one: the ground surveys, the plate archives, Gaia and the rest. Each series is a survey in one filter.

Flux - the space photometry that comes as flux rather than magnitudes, TESS and Kepler among them, each observing segment - a sector, a quarter, a campaign - normalized to its own median. Their absolute level is lost that way, but not the shape of the variation, which is what a segment of a few weeks is good for.

Time axis labels the light curve with calendar dates, in UT, or with Modified Julian Dates - the days the period tools below take their dates and T₀ in.

Switching the mode reloads the data; everything below - filters, folding, the period tools - works on whichever is shown.

Data Sources

One line per series - a survey in one filter, or an observing segment of a space mission - with its colour, its number of points, and a switch to show or hide it. What is hidden is left out of the statistics, the alignment, the period search and the downloads too.

Offset is added to the series as drawn, to bring surveys measured on different zero points together - by hand, or with Align offsets, which matches them where they overlap in time. Max Err, in magnitudes only, drops the points whose uncertainty is larger, and those with none. Size is that of the markers.

Only g shows only the series on the common g scale that the combined light curve is built from; Statistics gives the mean, median and scatter of each series over the points in view; Download writes the points shown as CSV.
Colour

The colour of the star as the difference of two sources measured at the same time - their medians in every time bin both have points in, Time bin days long. The bin has to be shorter than the time the star changes on, or its change reads as colour. The magnitudes are taken as measured, without the offsets of the display, and as filtered by the maximum errors.

The colour is drawn against time under the light curve, and follows it as it is zoomed with Same range; when the light curve is folded, it is folded too, with the binned median if that is shown above.

Diagram adds the colour against a magnitude - or, with a second colour chosen, against that colour. Connect in time joins its points in the order they were taken, to follow how the star moved through it; Fit line draws the straight line through them, whose slope is in the title. Along that line the star moved, and whether it is the reddening of dust or a change of temperature is in the value of the slope.

Only magnitudes have a colour, so the panel is hidden in flux mode.

Phase Folding

A period and T₀ fold the light curve, drawn from phase -0.6 to 1.6 so that a cycle is seen whole either side; 0 turns folding off. ×2 and ÷2 try the period's double and half - an eclipsing binary found at half its period, or a double wave at twice. Binned median draws each series' median in bins of phase, its shape at the period folded at.

Fit Period searches the visible sources, as filtered by their maximum errors and limited to the Dates, over the search range, each series about its own median. Lomb-Scargle fits a sinusoid, and with more harmonics a light curve of another shape - a pulsator's sawtooth, a contact binary's double wave; box least squares looks for a dip once a cycle, an eclipse or a transit. Remove trend fits each series a smooth curve with a knot every window days alongside, so that a short period can be found underneath a long variation; the window must be several times the period sought. No knots are closer than the visits allow, and isolated points - single Gaia or Pan-STARRS epochs - share one slow trend rather than each taking its own level, and with it all of its variation. The light curve is then shown with the trend that was fitted along with the period.

The highest peaks are fitted again - each series with its own trend, each survey and filter with its own harmonics - and ranked by that fit, which is what tells apart the aliases the periodogram gives the same power. Their buttons fold at them; hovering one gives its power, uncertainty and how much of the variation it explains. The uncertainties allow for noise that does not average down as white noise would.

Under the periodogram, the period folded at is marked, with its daily aliases, and the periodogram of the sampling alone centred on it: wherever that rises, the same signal is seen again through the sampling. Click anywhere on the periodogram to fold there.

Remove folded period takes the signal of the period folded at off the light curve and searches again - prewhitening, for a star with more than one period. Every removed period is fitted again with the others each time, and the light curve is shown as the residuals until they are cleared. What the last search said is kept below the periodogram, to copy or download.

         Time-Resolved Periodogram
      
      
        
Lomb-Scargle periodograms taken in a window sliding along the light curve, drawn as power against period and time - for how a period, or which of several, comes and goes, or drifts.

        
The points are those Fit Period searches - the visible sources, error filters, dates and trend, with any removed periods taken off - over the same search range. The power of each is the share of the variance in its window that a sinusoid at that period explains, so windows with more or fewer points compare alike; hovering gives how many each has.

        
Window is the days of data in each periodogram, a tenth of the span by default: periods are resolved no finer than about P²/window, and searched only up to half the window. The windows follow one another every so many days, a quarter of the window by default. Click the map to fold at a period.

         Period Change
      
      
        
A period that changes with time, fitted to the light curve itself: the phase is the integral of a frequency that is a polynomial in time of the given order - 0 for a constant period, 1 for one changing at a constant rate - or a spline with a knot every so many days, for a period that wanders - kept smooth where no data say otherwise, so that across a gap it bridges the period from one side to the other rather than swinging about.

        
Fit Period Change starts from the period being folded at, and uses it with T₀ as the reference ephemeris of the O−C diagram, moving T₀ to the nearest maximum of light. It uses the same visible sources, error filters, dates and trend as Fit Period. Counting the cycles across seasonal gaps is what such a fit depends on, so it is started in several ways - from the phases of windows that follow the seasons, and from the periods found within each - and the one that fits best is kept.

        
With O−C from light curve windows, the model is fitted to the light curve itself, its shape described by the number of harmonics chosen - three suit most pulsators and are quick, while the narrow eclipses of a detached binary need a dozen and more, which fewer put hours out. Auto takes as many as the Bayesian information criterion asks for. Each survey's errors are scaled to its scatter about the fit, and the fit made again, so that a few points with tiny errors that no model follows do not outweigh the thousands that count the cycles. The O−C is measured in windows of O−C window days, each fitted with that shape, shifted and scaled - by the surveys seen outside the window as well, as one seen in it alone fits any shift. The fit is then started once more from where the windows put the phase, and with whole cycles more or fewer across the widest gap between them; the summary says when another count there fits about as well.

        
With O−C from eclipse minima, every eclipse covered well enough - points along both branches and across the bottom, as the space missions have them - is timed on its own by a curve symmetric about its minimum, and the model is fitted to those times, primaries at whole cycles and secondaries half a cycle on, with their offset from it fitted too, as an eccentric orbit shifts them. The errors of each series' primaries and secondaries are scaled to their scatter, and minima far off the fit are left out, drawn hollow. T₀ is then a primary minimum.

        
The O−C is drawn with the model and its band, the residuals from it, and the period of the model with time beside the period each window finds on its own - the one check of the cycle count that does not depend on it. A window with too few points, or too aliased, to decide its period - one that fits several about as well - is drawn hollow, without an error bar. The errors allow for noise that does not average down as white noise would, and the summary says when the O−C wanders beyond them, as on a spotted star it will.

        
Fold with model folds the light curve with the model's phase rather than a constant period.
