This is a simple web-based tool for a quick-look of lightcurves for your object available in various archives. You give it a name or a position, and it collects whatever photometry, spectra and catalogue data the surveys hold for it.

It currently allows you to:

  • Resolve a target by name or by coordinates, and gather what SIMBAD, AAVSO VSX and Gaia DR3 know of it — identifiers, distance, kinematics, and the interstellar reddening along the line of sight
  • Acquire long-term lightcurves from twenty time-domain surveys, from Hipparcos and the photographic plates of DASCH and APPLAUSE to ZTF, TESS and NEOWISE
  • Keep every band as the survey published it, with conversions onto a common scale added as extra series rather than replacing anything, and say how strictly each source should judge its own measurements
  • Look at all of them together in an interactive viewer, with per-source offsets, and search for a period with a multiband Lomb-Scargle periodogram
  • Fetch archival spectra from SDSS, LAMOST, DESI, APOGEE, SPHEREx and the ESO archive, together with the Gaia XP and RVS ones, and view them on a single physical scale
  • Build a spectral energy distribution out of whatever VizieR catalogues cover the target, from the ultraviolet to the mid-infrared
  • Browse, preview and download everything that was written for a target

The Cutouts and Passbands pages are standalone utilities, open to everyone. The first previews multi-wavelength images of any sky position from a number of surveys; the second draws the photometric passbands the conversions run between, and lets the conversions themselves be calculated.

LCServer is being developed by Sergey Karpov. Its source code is available on GitHub.
