Every SED fit here interpolates one of these grids of model atmospheres and reports six numbers. What the grid itself says - how a colour runs with temperature, where two sets of models disagree, which bands a grid can answer for at all - decides whether those numbers mean anything, and is not visible anywhere else. The magnitudes below are computed with the same pivot wavelengths, zero points and extinction law the fit puts an observed magnitude through, so what is plotted here and what is fitted there are the same arithmetic.

A grid knows three parameters - effective temperature, surface gravity and metallicity - and no radius, so an absolute magnitude cannot be had from it alone. Everything here is a star of one solar radius seen from ten parsecs; a star of radius R sits 5 log10(R/R☉) brighter and no colour moves at all, which is exactly what the radius slider does.

Coverage and support which bands each grid predicts, and how densely its models are laid out

A band a grid has no flux for is one a fit silently drops, and the grids differ by more than thirty bands in what they reach. Where the models are is the other half of the same question: a scattered grid is interpolated over a triangulation of the models it has, and the hull of that triangulation is convex where a set of computed models is not - so past the temperatures a given gravity was computed at, a simplex carries on across a gap. Those are refused rather than answered, and the fraction of them is how much of a grid's apparent extent is not there.

Blue is a band the grid predicts and grey one it has no flux for. Amber is neither: the cube holds a perfectly good flux and no magnitude can be made of it, because the band lies past the red end of the reference Vega spectrum and so has no zero point to be read against. Herschel SPIRE PMW and PLW are the two such bands, and a measurement in either has to be given as a flux rather than as a magnitude.

What these numbers are not

  • Not a Hertzsprung-Russell diagram. No cube here carries a radius or a luminosity, so neither can be plotted. The colour-magnitude preset is the honest substitute: magnitudes at one solar radius, with the radius slider moving every one of them by the same 5 log10(R/R☉). A real track through the diagram would need evolutionary models, which are not part of this.
  • The PoWR grids do not share these axes. Their second parameter is log Rt, a transformed radius rather than a gravity, and their temperature is quoted at a reference optical depth in an expanding wind. A bolometric correction or an absolute magnitude computed from σT4 per unit surface is not referred to the same radius as it is for a photosphere, so those two axes are not comparable across the two kinds of grid. Colours are.
  • Synthetic colours carry the usual systematics. The solar node of Castelli & Kurucz comes out at MV 4.83, Mbol 4.76 and BCV -0.07, which are the accepted values; its B - V is 0.695 against an observed 0.65. Good enough to trace a dependence, not good enough to calibrate against.
  • Reddening is applied at the pivot wavelength, not integrated through the passband - Ab = Av·k(λpivot) on the Fitzpatrick (1999) law at RV = 3.1. That is an approximation, and it is the same one the likelihood makes, which is the reason for making it here.
